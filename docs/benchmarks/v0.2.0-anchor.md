# The v0.2.0 ARA anchor

**Status: complete (2026-11-13). The anchor MISSES.** ARA's routing load is
below AODV's at every pause time, in packets, in bytes and as NRL, and the
gated NRL check passes. Its delivery is below AODV's at pause 0 and 30 s, so
both gated PDR checks fail. The pre-registered rule needs all three. Org rule
6 counts a comparator's numbers only after its three gates, so ARA's v0.2.0
ships with this written infeasibility verdict, decided on
[#5](https://github.com/swarm-routing/ARA/issues/5) (maintainer decision
2026-11-13). Its delivery numbers are not presented as a reproduction of the
paper. The 2003 version, a different source with its own trend, is the next
anchor ([#27](https://github.com/swarm-routing/ARA/issues/27),
[#125](https://github.com/swarm-routing/ARA/issues/125), v0.3.0).

## What was fixed before dispatch

Each plan was committed before its first dispatch. `tools/bench/check_plan_deps.py`
checked it right before dispatch and again on the collected cells (#105).

- **Source.** Güneş, Sorges & Bouazizi, *ARA – The Ant-Colony Based Routing
  Algorithm for MANETs*, IWAHN 2002 ([G02],
  <https://doi.org/10.1109/icppw.2002.1039715>), §4.2, Figs. 4, 6, 8. The
  trend: at high mobility, ARA delivers more than AODV and has the lowest
  routing overhead.
- **Cell.** `routing-compare --scenario=paper`: 50 nodes, 1500 m × 300 m,
  802.11 at 2 Mbit/s, 300 m disk range, random waypoint at 1–10 m/s, 900 s,
  10 CBR flows of 64 B at 1 packet/s. Pauses 0, 30, 120 and 600 s, four of
  the paper's seven, as in AntHocNet v2.5.0. ARA and stock ns-3 AODV run in
  one invocation per seed, RngRun 1–20 per cell in two blocks of 10. The image
  is `ns3-dev:3.48-optimized` by digest (`sha256:5585a93…`); 3.48 because
  ns-3.49's AODV livelocks (swarm-routing/ns3-containers#35).
- **Deviations.** The fidelity sheet's "Anchor candidate" lists them:
  - data rate, radio range, minimum speed and traffic are repo choices from
    v2.5.0's anchor;
  - DSDV and DSR are not run (the org has no DSR arm);
  - 20 seeds, not the paper's 10.
- **Rule** (AntHocNet v2.5.0's, re-used: #4, decided 2026-11-09 and
  2026-11-10). ARA minus AODV, paired by seed:
  - the PDR 95 % CI lies wholly above zero at pause 0 and at pause 30;
  - the NRL 95 % CI lies wholly below zero at pause 0;
  - 120 and 600 s are reported, not gated.

## Threats to validity

The verdict is a miss under the rule as pre-registered. These are the reasons
it may not speak to ARA's design as a whole:

1. **The authors revised the 2002 version.** Their 2003 chapter is Günes &
   Spaniol, *Ant-Routing-Algorithm for Mobile Multi-Hop Ad-Hoc Networks*, IFIP
   Net-Con 2003, pp. 120–138, <https://doi.org/10.1007/978-0-387-35703-4_9>.
   It calls the 2002 algorithm (its ref. [6], [G02]) "ANT" and says it
   "performed poorly in highly mobile scenarios". The chapter adds five
   features:
   - continual pheromone decrease;
   - prioritized ants;
   - flooded BANTs;
   - a send buffer with minimal FANTs;
   - a MAC-Tap.

   Against AODV it reports delivery alike (both above DSR at high mobility)
   and routing overhead lowest at high mobility. It makes no claim that ARA
   delivers more than AODV. `core/` implements [G02] (#15), the version the
   authors themselves revised.
2. **The 2002 AODV baseline.** [G02] compares against an ns-2 AODV from
   draft-ietf-manet-aodv-07 (2000). That AODV delivers under 95 % except at
   pause times above 600 s. The study whose parameters [G02] takes, Broch et
   al. 1998 ([BMJ98]), reports its AODV variant "delivering over 95% of the
   data packets regardless of mobility rate". Stock ns-3.48 AODV is a
   present-day AODV, closer to the stronger baseline.
3. **What the switch covers.** AntHocNet v2.5.0's `ara` arm passed this rule,
   on ns-3.42 with 10 seeds (fidelity sheet, "Anchor candidate"). That arm
   mixed in 2003 behaviour: probabilistic forwarding, continuous decay and a
   hold queue. `core/` already decays continuously, a stated deviation in the
   fidelity sheet's evaporation row. `BufferDuringDiscovery` restores the hold
   queue, without minimal FANTs, and it closes part of the gap (pause 0,
   anchor-3 against anchor-buffer, above). These 2003 features are not
   implemented:
   - statistical (probabilistic) path selection: data takes the highest ϕ, as
     2002 does;
   - prioritized ants;
   - flooded BANTs;
   - minimal FANTs;
   - the MAC-Tap.
4. **Scenario.** These choices are the fidelity sheet's repo choices, inherited
   from v2.5.0's anchor:
   - 300 m disk range and 64 B at 1 packet/s, where [BMJ98] has 250 m two-ray
     and 4 packets/s, and the 2003 chapter labels its traffic 4 × 512 B;
   - four of the paper's seven pause times;
   - no DSDV or DSR arm.

## What follows

- **ARA v0.2.0 ships with this verdict.** `ara` stays in the per-PR delivery
  smoke. No page quotes its delivery against AODV as a reproduction of [G02].
  Its lower routing load is reported, with this page.
- **`BufferDuringDiscovery` stays off by default** (maintainer decision
  2026-11-13). It does not pass the delivery gate, and it costs tail delay
  (the A/B above).
- **v0.3.0:** [#27](https://github.com/swarm-routing/ARA/issues/27) reviews the
  sheet against the 2003 sources. Then
  [#125](https://github.com/swarm-routing/ARA/issues/125) puts the 2003
  features behind default-off switches, and a new pre-registered anchor
  follows from the 2003 chapter's own trend: delivery about equal to AODV, routing overhead lowest
  at high mobility. A different source is a new anchor, never a re-reading of
  this one.
